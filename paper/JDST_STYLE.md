# Submission conventions

The papers are written to the conventions of the Journal of Diabetes Science and Technology.
The prose rules are unchanged: continuous argument rather than lists, no dashes, no bold for
emphasis, no account of how a draft came to say what it says. What follows is the apparatus a
submission carries on top of that.

## Order

Title. Byline. Structured abstract. Keywords. Abbreviations. Introduction. Methods. Results.
Discussion. Conclusion. Declarations. References where the argument rests on prior work.

## The structured abstract

Four labelled parts, Background, Methods, Results and Conclusions, each a short paragraph and
the whole under 250 words. Background states the problem and why it is open. Methods states
the design, the data and the analysis, including the number of participants and the span.
Results carries the numbers that decide the question, with intervals. Conclusions states what
follows and, where the design does not support a causal reading, says so in the same sentence
rather than in a later caveat.

## Keywords and abbreviations

Five to eight keywords, drawn from the vocabulary a reader would search: automated insulin
delivery, continuous glucose monitoring, hypoglycaemia, type 1 diabetes, and whatever the
paper is specifically about. Abbreviations are expanded at first use in the body regardless,
and the list carries only those used more than once.

## Declarations

Four statements, written accurately rather than conventionally.

Funding. None was received.

Conflicting interests. The work concerns an algorithm the authors develop and use, which is
declared plainly because it is the relevant conflict.

Ethical approval. These are retrospective analyses of data generated by people using an
open-source automated insulin delivery system of their own choosing, contributed for
analysis. No interventional protocol was applied, nothing was randomised, and no institutional
review board reviewed the work. Where a paper reports a change made to the algorithm during
the period it covers, that change was made by the participants to their own systems. This is
stated as a limitation on inference rather than presented as approval.

Data availability. The analysis scripts are in the algorithm repository. The underlying
records are personal health data and are not published.

## Numbers

Glucose in mg/dL with mmol/L alongside where a threshold is being defined. Intervals given as
95 per cent, with the resampling unit named. A point estimate without an interval is not
reported. Time in range and time below range are given to one decimal place, effect sizes to
the precision the design supports and no further.

## What does not change

The identification constraint is stated wherever a policy claim is made: without a
glucodynamic simulator there is no counterfactual trajectory, so a comparison of what was
delivered is not a comparison of what would have happened. Papers that price a policy say
this in the Discussion, once, and continue.
